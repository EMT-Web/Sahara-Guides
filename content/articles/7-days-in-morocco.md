Before choosing a route, accept one fact about Moroccan geography: **the Sahara is far from everywhere**. Merzouga, at the edge of the Erg Chebbi dunes, is around 560 km by road from Marrakech and 470 km from Fes, across one or two mountain ranges. Every good one-week itinerary is built around that distance.

The three itineraries below are designed for different travellers. Each lists overnight stops, realistic driving times (typical times without stops; add 30–60 minutes per day for photos and lunch) and the alternatives if something does not suit you.

| | Route 1: Classic desert crossing | Route 2: Mountains and coast | Route 3: Imperial cities by train |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | First trips, couples, photographers | Families, slower travellers, summer | Culture lovers, no car |
| Start / end | Marrakech → Fes (one way) | Marrakech → Marrakech | Casablanca → Marrakech |
| Sahara night | Yes, Erg Chebbi | No | No |
| Total road distance | About 1,100 km | About 500 km | Train only |
| Longest day | 7.5–8 h (Merzouga–Fes) | About 4 h | About 7 h by train (Fes–Marrakech) |

## Route 1: Marrakech to Fes via the Sahara (the classic)

This is the route most people picture when they think of Morocco: a medina, a mountain pass, an earthen fortress, a gorge and a night under the stars in the dunes. Doing it one way, Marrakech to Fes, avoids driving the same road twice.

![](kasbah-palm-grove)

### Day 1: Arrive in Marrakech

Land at Marrakech Menara airport (RAK), about 20 minutes from the medina, and check in to a riad. Spend the late afternoon getting oriented: walk to the Koutoubia Mosque, then into **Jemaa el-Fna** as the evening food stalls set up. Our [Marrakech guide](/destinations/marrakech) has neighbourhood and riad advice.

**Overnight:** Marrakech.

### Day 2: Marrakech

A full day. In the morning, with a licensed guide if it is your first visit, see the **Bahia Palace**, the **Saadian Tombs** and the **Ben Youssef Madrasa**, and walk through the souks. In the afternoon, visit **Jardin Majorelle** (book a timed ticket online) and rest before dinner.

**Overnight:** Marrakech.

### Day 3: Marrakech to Ait Ben Haddou

**About 185 km, 3.5–4 hours.** The road climbs over the High Atlas by the **Tizi n'Tichka pass** (2,260 m), with views over terraced Berber villages. A short detour after the pass leads to the Glaoui kasbah at Telouet, worth an hour if you leave early. Arrive at **Ait Ben Haddou** by mid-afternoon and walk up through the ksar to the granary at the top for sunset.

Staying overnight here, rather than in Ouarzazate, means you see the ksar without day-trip crowds.

**Overnight:** Ait Ben Haddou.

### Day 4: Ait Ben Haddou to the Dades Valley

**About 150 km, 3 hours.** Drive through Ouarzazate and the oasis of **Skoura**, with its palm groves and kasbahs, then along the "Road of a Thousand Kasbahs" through **Kelaat M'Gouna**, centre of Morocco's rose-growing country. Continue into the **Dades Gorge** to see the famous switchback road from the viewpoint above it.

**Overnight:** Dades Valley (guesthouses inside the gorge have the best views).

### Day 5: Dades Valley to Erg Chebbi

**About 260 km, 4.5 hours.** Stop at the **Todra Gorge** near Tinghir, where a short walk takes you between walls of rock that close in to a narrow passage. Continue east via Tinejdad and Erfoud to **Merzouga**, arriving in the afternoon. Leave your main luggage in the vehicle or at a hotel, and ride a camel (about an hour) or take a 4x4 to your **desert camp** for sunset from the dunes.

**Overnight:** Desert camp in Erg Chebbi.

> [!TIP]
> Pack a small overnight bag for the camp: warm layers (even in spring), a head torch, toothbrush, any medication and a scarf against sand. See [how to get to the Sahara](/sahara-desert/how-to-get-to-the-sahara-desert) for what to expect at camp.

### Day 6: Erg Chebbi to Fes

**About 470 km, 7.5–8 hours.** Wake early for sunrise from the top of a dune, then ride back to Merzouga for breakfast and a shower. The drive north follows the **Ziz Valley**, with its palm-filled gorges, then climbs through Midelt and the **Middle Atlas** cedar forests near Azrou, where Barbary macaques are often seen by the road, and passes through the alpine-style town of **Ifrane**. Arrive in Fes in the evening.

This is the longest day of the trip. It is manageable with an early start and stops every two hours; it is not one to drive yourself after a sleepless night in camp.

**Overnight:** Fes.

### Day 7: Fes

Spend the day in **Fes el-Bali**, ideally with a licensed guide: the Bou Inania and al-Attarine madrasas, the exterior of the al-Qarawiyyin mosque and university, the Chouara tannery seen from a leather-shop terrace, and the craft quarters. Fly home from Fes-Saïss airport (FEZ) in the evening or the next morning, or take the train to Casablanca (about 3.5–4 hours) for a flight from Mohammed V (CMN). See our [Fes guide](/destinations/fes).

### Variations on route 1

- **Start in Fes instead.** Fly into Fes, spend two nights, then drive Fes → Merzouga (7.5–8 h) on day three. Reverse the rest and finish in Marrakech, which has more international flights.
- **Two nights in the desert.** Drop a Marrakech night and stay one night in a Merzouga hotel before the camp. It makes day 5 calmer and gives you a full day in the dunes.
- **Must return to Marrakech?** Do not drive Merzouga to Marrakech (about 560 km, 9.5–10 hours) in one go if you can avoid it. Break it with a night in the Dades Valley or Ouarzazate, and cut the Fes day instead.

## Route 2: Marrakech, the High Atlas and Essaouira

A week with little driving and plenty of variety: the city, a mountain village and the Atlantic. It suits families, travellers who dislike long days in a car and summer trips, when the coast and mountains are far more comfortable than the desert.

### Day 1–2: Marrakech

As in route 1. With children, add the **Le Jardin Secret** garden and an evening on Jemaa el-Fna to watch the storytellers and musicians (agree any photo payment first).

### Day 3: Marrakech to Imlil

**About 65 km, 1.5 hours.** Drive south through the Asni valley to **Imlil**, at about 1,740 m in the High Atlas. In the afternoon, walk with a local mountain guide to the villages of the Imlil valley, or to the shrine of Sidi Chamharouch if you are fit (a full half-day). Evenings are cool, even in summer.

**Overnight:** Imlil (a guesthouse with views of the Toubkal massif).

### Day 4: Imlil to Essaouira

**About 250 km, 4 hours.** A longer day, back past Marrakech and west across the plains, with argan trees appearing as you near the coast. Arrive in **Essaouira**, a walled 18th-century port, in time for a walk on the ramparts at sunset.

**Overnight:** Essaouira.

### Day 5: Essaouira

Explore the medina's galleries and woodwork shops, watch the blue fishing boats come in at the harbour and eat grilled fish at the port stalls. Afternoon options include a kitesurfing lesson, a horse or camel ride along the beach, or simply a long lunch.

**Overnight:** Essaouira.

### Day 6: Essaouira to Marrakech

**About 190 km, 2.5–3 hours.** Stop at a women's argan cooperative on the way. Spend the afternoon on anything you missed in Marrakech, or book a hammam.

**Overnight:** Marrakech.

## Route 3: Imperial cities by train

Morocco's railway network links the four imperial cities, and the trains are comfortable and inexpensive. This route needs no car and no long road days. The trade-off is no mountains or desert.

| Day | Plan | Train time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive Casablanca. Hassan II Mosque guided tour and the Art Deco centre | |
| 2 | Train to Rabat. Kasbah of the Udayas, Hassan Tower, Chellah | About 1 hour |
| 3 | Train to Fes. Evening at a medina rooftop | About 3 hours |
| 4 | Fes el-Bali with a licensed guide | |
| 5 | Day trip to Meknes, Volubilis and Moulay Idriss (by car with driver or train plus taxi) | About 45 minutes to Meknes by train |
| 6 | Train to Marrakech | About 7 hours |

> [!NOTE]
> The Fes to Marrakech train is long but direct. Book first class for more space, and take food and water. A faster alternative is a private transfer, which takes around 7–8 hours by road, so the train is usually the better option.

Check current timetables on the [ONCF website](https://www.oncf.ma/en) before booking; times vary between services. Our [guide to getting around Morocco](/transportation/getting-around-morocco) explains tickets and classes.

## Getting around: driver, tour or self-drive?

- **Private driver.** The most popular option for routes 1 and 2. The driver handles the mountain roads and the logistics, and you can stop when you like. Book with a licensed transport company, and agree what is included (fuel, the driver's accommodation, camp transfers).
- **Small-group tour.** Shared tours from Marrakech to the desert and Fes are the cheapest option for route 1, but the timetable is fixed and the stops are short.
- **Self-drive.** Possible for confident drivers. Main roads are paved all the way to Merzouga. Avoid night driving and leave the car at Merzouga when you go to camp.

## Mistakes to avoid on a one-week trip

1. **Adding Chefchaouen to the desert loop.** It is a 3.5–4 hour drive north of Fes, which adds at least one full day each way. Save it for a 10-day trip or a separate northern itinerary.
2. **A desert day trip from Marrakech.** It does not exist. The Agafay "desert" near Marrakech is a stony plain, pleasant for an evening but not the Sahara.
3. **Scheduling a flight on the day you leave the desert.** Leave at least one night between camp and an international flight.
4. **Travelling in midsummer without adapting.** In July and August, choose route 2 or shift the desert to a cooler season. See [the best time to visit Morocco](/travel-tips/best-time-to-visit-morocco).

## Which route should you choose?

If the Sahara is a must, choose **route 1**. If you are travelling with young children, in high summer or simply want a calmer week, choose **route 2**. If you prefer trains, history and food to scenery, choose **route 3**. And if you have a different starting city or more days, the [itinerary planner](/itinerary-planner) will suggest a realistic alternative.
