Morocco sits at the meeting point of three climates. The Atlantic keeps the west coast mild all year; the Atlas Mountains bring snow in winter and cool air in summer; and south of the mountains, the edge of the Sahara swings between hot days and cold nights. That is why the answer to "when should I go?" depends on your route, not just the calendar.

For a typical first trip (a few days in Marrakech or Fes, a drive over the High Atlas and a night in the dunes), **spring (March to May) and autumn (late September to November) are the most comfortable seasons**. Everything below explains why, month by month, and what changes if your plans are different.

## Morocco's climate in one paragraph

Coastal Morocco has a mild, Mediterranean-to-Atlantic climate: Essaouira rarely sees summer highs above the mid-20s °C. The inland plains around Marrakech and Fes are hot and dry in summer and pleasantly mild in winter, with most rain falling between November and April. The High Atlas is a true mountain range, with peaks above 4,000 metres and regular winter snow. South of the mountains the climate is arid: the region around Errachidia receives only about 140 mm of rain a year, and Merzouga, at the edge of Erg Chebbi, regularly reaches around 40°C in midsummer.

## The best time to visit, by region

### Marrakech and the imperial cities

**Best: March to May, October to November.** Spring brings orange blossom, green fields around Fes and Meknes, and comfortable walking weather in the medinas. Autumn is similar but drier. Winter (December to February) is perfectly good for city sightseeing, with sunny days of around 18–20°C in Marrakech, but evenings are cool and many traditional riads, built around open courtyards, are hard to heat. In July and August, plan your walking for the early morning and the evening; the middle of the day is genuinely hot.

### The Sahara: Erg Chebbi and Erg Chigaga

**Best: October to April**, with the sweet spots in **October–November** and **March–April**. Daytime temperatures are pleasant for a camel ride, and the evening around the fire is comfortable. December to February are often clear and still, with spectacular night skies, but you will want warm layers, a hat and gloves; well-run camps provide heavy blankets. Spring can bring wind and sandstorms, which usually last hours rather than days.

From **June to August**, temperatures around 40°C make the desert hard work. Some travellers still go, keeping the camel ride for late afternoon and the walk back for sunrise, but it is not the trip most people imagine. See our guide to [choosing between Erg Chebbi and Erg Chigaga](/sahara-desert/erg-chebbi-vs-erg-chigaga) for how the season affects each.

### The High Atlas and Toubkal

**Best for walking: April to June and September to October.** Valley walks around Imlil are pleasant for much of the year. From roughly November to April, the higher slopes and Jbel Toubkal (4,167 m) are under snow and become a winter mountaineering trip that needs crampons, an ice axe and an experienced mountain guide. Snow can also slow or temporarily close the Tizi n'Tichka pass between Marrakech and Ouarzazate, usually for hours rather than days.

### The Atlantic coast

**Best: May to October.** Essaouira, Agadir and the surf villages north of Agadir stay mild in summer, which makes them the natural escape from the heat inland. Essaouira is famously windy from spring to late summer: excellent for kitesurfing, less ideal for lying on the beach. Agadir has the most reliable sunshine and the calmest beach weather of the main resorts. Winter on the coast is mild, with highs in the high teens, and is the main surf season.

### Chefchaouen and the north

**Best: April to June, September to October.** The Rif mountains receive more rain than the rest of the country, mostly between November and March, and winter evenings in Chefchaouen are cold. Spring is lush and green, ideal for walking in Talassemtane National Park.

## Ramadan: what to expect if your trip overlaps

Ramadan follows the Islamic lunar calendar, so it moves about 11 days earlier each year. **Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around 8 February and end around 9 March 2027**, with Eid al-Fitr on about 9 or 10 March. Morocco confirms the start and end by local moon sighting, so the dates can shift by a day.

Travelling during Ramadan is entirely possible, and many visitors find it a rewarding time to be in Morocco. What changes:

- **Daytime is quieter.** Many cafés and local restaurants close until sunset, though hotels, riads and many tourist restaurants continue to serve food. Shops and sights generally stay open, sometimes with shorter hours.
- **Sunset is the event of the day.** Streets empty just before the *iftar* (the meal that breaks the fast), then fill again in the evening, which is lively and sociable.
- **Be considerate.** Avoid eating, drinking or smoking openly in the street during daylight. Drivers and guides who are fasting may tire by late afternoon, so plan long drives with that in mind.
- **Eid al-Fitr** at the end of the month is a family holiday. Expect busy roads and buses either side of it, and some businesses closed for a day or two.

> [!TIP]
> If you are visiting during Ramadan, accept an invitation to share an iftar if one is offered. The traditional table usually includes *harira* soup, dates, eggs and sweet *chebakia* pastries.

## Festivals and events worth timing a trip around

Dates are set by the organisers each year and some move with the lunar calendar, so check the official announcement before you book.

- **Rose Festival, Kelaat M'Gouna** (usually May): a harvest celebration in the Dades Valley, where Damask roses are grown for rosewater.
- **Fes Festival of World Sacred Music** (usually late spring or early summer): concerts in the city's historic venues.
- **Gnaoua and World Music Festival, Essaouira** (usually June): free outdoor concerts mixing Gnawa musicians with international artists. Accommodation fills months in advance.
- **Imilchil marriage festival** (usually September): a gathering of Ait Haddidou communities in the eastern High Atlas, with a large market.
- **Marrakech International Film Festival** (usually late November or early December).

## Peak seasons, prices and crowds

Prices for riads and desert camps rise sharply at **Christmas and New Year** and around **Easter**, when European visitors arrive in large numbers. **August** is the peak of domestic tourism and of Moroccans living abroad returning home, so the coast, ferries and roads to the north are busy. The quietest good-weather windows are **late January to mid-March** (outside Ramadan if you prefer normal opening hours) and **November**.

## Common mistakes

- **Packing for "Africa" in winter.** December nights in Fes, the Atlas and the desert are cold. Bring a warm layer and a hat.
- **Underestimating summer.** A full day in the Fes medina in August, then a long drive to the desert, is exhausting. Build in slow afternoons.
- **Assuming the Atlas roads are always open.** In winter, snow can close the Tizi n'Tichka or the Middle Atlas roads for a few hours. Leave a buffer before an international flight.
- **Forgetting the moving calendar.** Ramadan and the Eid holidays change every year; check them as soon as you know your travel month.

## The bottom line

If you can choose freely, go in **April or October**. If you can only travel in summer, combine the coast and the mountains with a short, early-season desert visit, or skip the desert until a cooler trip. And if winter suits you, Morocco's south rewards you with clear light and quieter roads; just pack for cold nights.
