Ask ten people who have been to the Moroccan Sahara which dunes are best and you will hear passionate arguments for both. The truth is that they suit different travellers. The most useful question is not "which is more beautiful?" (both are spectacular) but "how much time do I have, and what kind of desert experience do I want?"

## The quick comparison

| | Erg Chebbi | Erg Chigaga |
|---|---|---|
| Nearest village | Merzouga | M'Hamid El Ghizlane |
| Last section of the journey | Paved road to the edge of the dunes | About 60 km of desert track, 4x4 only |
| From Marrakech | About 560 km, 9.5–10 h | About 450 km, 7.5–8 h, plus 1.5–2.5 h off-road |
| From Fes | About 470 km, 7.5–8 h | Not practical as a direct trip |
| Nearest airport | Errachidia (ERH), about 2 h | Ouarzazate (OZZ), about 4.5 h plus off-road; Zagora (OZG) closer but few flights |
| Dunes | Tall (around 150 m), concentrated | Larger area, generally lower dunes |
| Crowds | Popular; quieter away from Merzouga | Few camps; a much greater sense of space |
| Camps | Hundreds, from basic to luxury | Fewer, mostly mid-range to upmarket |
| Best for | First visits, families, routes to or from Fes | Wilderness, photographers, repeat visitors |

## Erg Chebbi: tall dunes, easy access

Erg Chebbi rises abruptly from the stony plain east of Merzouga: a band of orange dunes running north to south for roughly 20–30 km and a few kilometres wide, with some peaks around 150 metres high. Because paved roads run along its western edge, you can drive to a hotel at the foot of the dunes, leave your luggage, and ride a camel to camp in about an hour.

![](erg-chebbi-sunrise)

**Why choose Erg Chebbi**

- **It fits more itineraries.** It sits on the classic route between Marrakech and Fes, so you can cross the country without retracing your steps.
- **Taller, photogenic dunes.** The big dunes give sweeping views at sunrise and sunset.
- **More choice and better value.** Camps range from simple Berber tents to upmarket camps with private bathrooms. Hotels and auberges along the edge make it easy to add a comfortable night before or after camp.
- **More to do nearby.** Gnawa music in the village of Khamlia, the market town of Rissani, fossil workshops around Erfoud, and, in rare wet years, seasonal lakes such as Dayet Srji that attract birds.
- **Easier with children or limited mobility.** Short transfers, and 4x4 access to many camps.

**What to know**

- The edge of the dunes near Merzouga can be busy, and quad bikes and 4x4s are noisy. Camps on the quieter sides of the erg, or deeper among the dunes, feel much more remote.
- Quality varies enormously between camps. Book a named camp with recent reviews, not "a desert camp".

## Erg Chigaga: the wilder Sahara

Erg Chigaga lies west of M'Hamid El Ghizlane, where the tarmac ends at the southern tip of the Draa Valley. The dunes are spread over a larger area than Erg Chebbi, and although the highest are generally lower, the landscape feels vast and empty. Getting there is part of the experience: a long drive through the palm groves of the Draa Valley, then a bumpy 4x4 crossing of open desert.

![](camels-under-stars)

**Why choose Erg Chigaga**

- **Solitude.** There are far fewer camps, spread widely. On many nights you will see only your own camp's lights.
- **A real sense of journey.** The off-road crossing, often past nomad encampments and acacia trees, feels like travelling into the desert rather than visiting it.
- **The Draa Valley on the way.** Morocco's longest river valley, lined with palm groves, kasbahs and ksour.
- **A loop is possible.** Exit west across the dry lake of Iriqui to Foum Zguid, then return to Ouarzazate on paved roads.

**What to know**

- **The off-road section is not optional.** It takes 1.5–2.5 hours each way by 4x4 with a driver who knows the desert. It is bumpy and dusty.
- **Fewer budget options.** Most camps are mid-range to upmarket, partly because supplies and water must be brought in by 4x4.
- **More time needed.** Two nights at camp is ideal to make the journey worthwhile.

## Which should you choose?

| If you… | Choose |
|---|---|
| Have only 2–3 days and are starting in Fes | Erg Chebbi |
| Want to cross from Marrakech to Fes (or the reverse) | Erg Chebbi |
| Are travelling with young children or grandparents | Erg Chebbi |
| Are on a tight budget | Erg Chebbi |
| Want the tallest dunes | Erg Chebbi |
| Want quiet, space and as few people as possible | Erg Chigaga |
| Are starting and ending in Marrakech or Ouarzazate, with 3–4 days | Erg Chigaga (or Erg Chebbi with 4 days) |
| Have visited Erg Chebbi before | Erg Chigaga |
| Love long drives and remote landscapes | Erg Chigaga |

## Seasons: the same rule for both

Both dune seas share a similar climate. **October to April** is the best period, with the most comfortable conditions in **October–November** and **March–April**. In **December to February** days are pleasant but nights can approach freezing, so check that your camp provides heavy blankets or heating. From **June to August**, daytime temperatures around 40°C make camel rides and walking hard work. Spring is the windiest season, with occasional sandstorms. See [the best time to visit Morocco](/travel-tips/best-time-to-visit-morocco) for month-by-month details.

## What about the smaller dunes?

You will see other dunes advertised. They can be lovely, but they are not substitutes for the two great ergs:

- **Erg Znaigui and Erg Lihoudi**, near M'Hamid: small dune fields that are much quicker to reach than Erg Chigaga. Good for a short visit, but less impressive.
- **Tinfou**, near Zagora: a small patch of dunes by the road, often used on two-day "desert" tours from Marrakech.
- **Agafay**, near Marrakech: not a sand desert at all, but a stony plateau with views of the Atlas. Pleasant for an evening, but not the Sahara.

> [!TIP]
> If a two-day "Sahara" trip from Marrakech sounds too good to be true, check the destination. Two days is not enough to reach Erg Chebbi or Erg Chigaga and back; these trips usually go to Zagora's small dunes.

## Choosing a camp, wherever you go

- **Ask for the camp's name** and read recent reviews.
- **Check the location:** how far from the village, and how you get there (camel, 4x4 or both).
- **Confirm what is included:** dinner and breakfast, private or shared bathroom, electricity, heating or blankets in winter.
- **Ask about responsible practices:** how waste and water are managed, and how the camels are cared for. Well-run camps rest their animals and do not overload them.

## The bottom line

For most first-time visitors, **Erg Chebbi** is the better choice: easier logistics, spectacular dunes and a route that flows naturally between Marrakech and Fes. If you want silence, space and a sense of adventure, and have the time for it, **Erg Chigaga** is unforgettable. Either way, read [how to get to the Sahara](/sahara-desert/how-to-get-to-the-sahara-desert) before you book.
