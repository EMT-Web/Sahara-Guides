Fes was founded at the end of the 8th century by Idris I and developed by his son Idris II, and it grew into one of the great intellectual centres of the medieval Islamic world. It was Morocco's capital several times. What makes it extraordinary today is how much of that city survives in daily use: mosques, madrasas, fountains, caravanserais and workshops where crafts are still made by hand, in a medina where goods are still moved by donkey and handcart.

## The three cities of Fes

Fes is really three cities:

- **Fes el-Bali** ("old Fes"): the walled medieval medina and the reason to visit. UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1981.
- **Fes el-Jdid** ("new Fes", though it dates from the 13th century): built by the Marinid dynasty, with the Royal Palace and the Mellah, the historic Jewish quarter.
- **The Ville Nouvelle**: the 20th-century French-built city, with the train station, modern hotels and cafés.

## What to see in Fes el-Bali

### Bab Bou Jeloud

The main western gate, faced with blue tiles on the outside and green on the inside, built in 1913. Most visitors enter the medina here, and the two main streets, Talaa Kebira and Talaa Seghira, run downhill from just inside.

### Bou Inania Madrasa

A Marinid religious school completed in the 14th century, with a courtyard of carved cedar, stucco and zellige that rivals any in Morocco. It is one of the few active religious buildings in the city that non-Muslims may enter.

### Al-Attarine Madrasa

A smaller but exquisitely decorated 14th-century madrasa next to the spice market, close to the al-Qarawiyyin. The craftsmanship of the tilework and carved plaster is at its finest here.

### Al-Qarawiyyin mosque and university

Founded in 859 by Fatima al-Fihri, the daughter of a merchant from Kairouan, the al-Qarawiyyin grew into one of the oldest continuously operating institutions of learning in the world. The mosque is closed to non-Muslims, but you can look into the courtyard through the doorways. Its library, one of the oldest in the world, was restored and partly opened to the public in recent years; ask locally about access.

### The Chouara tannery

Fes's most famous sight: a honeycomb of stone vats where hides are soaked and dyed by hand, much as they have been for centuries. You watch from the terraces of the surrounding leather shops. Shop staff will hand you a sprig of mint for the smell. Go in the morning, when the most work is happening.

### Craft quarters and fondouks

Walk through the coppersmiths' square of **Place Seffarine**, the dyers' souk by the river, and the weavers' workshops. The **Nejjarine Museum of Wooden Arts and Crafts**, in a restored 18th-century fondouk (caravanserai), has a rooftop café with medina views.

### Viewpoints

For the classic panorama over the medina, walk or take a taxi up to the **Merenid Tombs** or the **Borj Nord** fortress on the northern hill, ideally late in the afternoon. The call to prayer echoing across the valley at sunset is unforgettable.

## Fes el-Jdid and the Mellah

The **Royal Palace** (Dar el-Makhzen) is closed to visitors, but its monumental brass doors, set in zellige and carved cedar, are among the most photographed sights in Morocco. Next door, the **Mellah** has distinctive houses with wooden balconies, the restored **Ibn Danan Synagogue** and a large Jewish cemetery.

## How to handle the medina

Fes el-Bali is a dense web of lanes, many too narrow for two people to pass, and GPS is unreliable. It is also the city with the most persistent unofficial guides.

- **Book a licensed guide for your first morning.** Your riad can arrange one. Agree the duration, the price and whether shopping stops are included (say if you do not want any).
- **Use landmarks.** The two main streets run downhill from Bab Bou Jeloud to the al-Qarawiyyin; if lost, head uphill to return.
- **Download an offline map** and save your riad's location.
- **Decline unofficial guides firmly and politely** ("*la, shukran*"), and keep walking.
- **Step aside for donkeys and carts** when you hear "*balak!*" (watch out).

> [!TIP]
> Shops and many workshops close or slow down on Friday around midday prayers, and parts of the medina are quieter on Friday generally. Plan your souk visit for another day if you can.

## How many days to spend in Fes

| Time | What fits |
|---|---|
| 1 day | Guided morning in Fes el-Bali, the tanneries, sunset from the Merenid Tombs. Rushed. |
| 2 days | Fes el-Bali in depth, Fes el-Jdid, the Mellah, a cooking class or hammam. Ideal. |
| 3 days | Add Meknes, Volubilis and Moulay Idriss, or the Middle Atlas. |

## Where to stay

- **In Fes el-Bali**: riads near **Bab Bou Jeloud** or **Batha** combine atmosphere with easy taxi access at the medina's edge. Deeper in the medina, riads are quieter but harder to reach with luggage.
- **Riads with rooftop terraces** offer some of the best views in the city.
- **The Ville Nouvelle**: modern hotels, convenient for the train station, but a taxi ride from the medina.

## Getting there and away

- **By air:** Fes-Saïss airport (FEZ) is about 15 km south of the city, around 25 minutes by car. Pre-book a transfer or take an airport taxi.
- **By train:** ONCF trains run to Meknes (under an hour), Rabat (about 3 hours), Casablanca (about 3.5–4 hours) and Marrakech (about 7 hours). Tangier is reached by train with a connection to Al Boraq, or by road in around 3.5–4 hours.
- **By road:** Chefchaouen about 3.5–4 hours; Merzouga about 7.5–8 hours.

## Day trips from Fes

| Trip | Time each way | Highlights |
|---|---|---|
| Meknes | About 1 h | Bab Mansour, Moulay Ismail's granaries, a relaxed medina |
| Volubilis and Moulay Idriss | About 1–1.5 h | Roman mosaics, the holy town of Moulay Idriss |
| Ifrane and Azrou | About 1–1.5 h | Cedar forests, Barbary macaques, alpine-style Ifrane |
| Chefchaouen | 3.5–4 h | Better as an overnight trip |

Combining Meknes, Volubilis and Moulay Idriss in one day by car is popular and very manageable.

## Food in Fes

Fes is known as the capital of Moroccan cuisine. Try **pastilla** (bastilla), a flaky pie traditionally filled with pigeon or chicken, almonds and cinnamon, dusted with sugar; **bissara**, a fava bean soup eaten at breakfast from street stalls; and **khlii**, preserved dried meat. A cooking class in a riad is one of the best ways to understand the cuisine. More in our [Moroccan food guide](/culture-food/moroccan-food-guide).

## Common mistakes

- **Arriving without a riad pick-up.** Finding a medina riad alone at night with luggage is hard work.
- **Following the "free" tannery tour.** Visit the tanneries, but on your own terms or with your guide.
- **Trying to see Fes in an afternoon** on the way to somewhere else. It needs at least a full day.
- **Visiting in midsummer without adapting.** Fes is hot in July and August; plan early starts and long lunches.

## Best time to visit Fes

**March to May** and **late September to November** are ideal. Winters are mild by day but cold at night, with most rain between November and April; summers are hot, often above 35°C. See [the best time to visit Morocco](/travel-tips/best-time-to-visit-morocco).

## Suggested 2-day plan

- **Day 1:** Guided morning in Fes el-Bali: Bab Bou Jeloud, Bou Inania, al-Attarine, al-Qarawiyyin, the tanneries and Place Seffarine. Afternoon at the Nejjarine Museum; sunset from the Merenid Tombs.
- **Day 2:** Fes el-Jdid, the Royal Palace doors and the Mellah in the morning; afternoon cooking class or hammam; dinner on a riad terrace.
