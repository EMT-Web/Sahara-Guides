"The Sahara" covers a third of Africa, but for travellers in Morocco it usually means one of two great *ergs*, or dune seas, close to the Algerian border:

- **Erg Chebbi**, beside the village of **Merzouga** in the south-east. Easier to reach, with paved roads to the edge of the dunes and hundreds of camps.
- **Erg Chigaga**, beyond the village of **M'Hamid El Ghizlane** at the end of the Draa Valley. Larger and remote, reached only by 4x4 across desert tracks.

If you have not decided between them, read our [comparison of Erg Chebbi and Erg Chigaga](/sahara-desert/erg-chebbi-vs-erg-chigaga) first. This guide assumes Erg Chebbi unless stated, because that is where most travellers go.

## Distances and driving times to the dunes

Times below are typical driving times without stops, on paved roads, in good weather. A real day with breaks is 1–2 hours longer.

| From | To Merzouga (Erg Chebbi) | To M'Hamid (for Erg Chigaga) |
|---|---|---|
| Marrakech | 560 km · 9.5–10 h | 450 km · 7.5–8 h, then 1.5–2.5 h by 4x4 |
| Fes | 470 km · 7.5–8 h | Not practical in one day |
| Casablanca | Via Marrakech or Fes · 11–12 h | Via Marrakech · 10–11 h |
| Ouarzazate | 370 km · 6–6.5 h | 260 km · 4.5 h |
| Errachidia | 130 km · 2 h | Not practical |
| Agadir | About 740 km · 12+ h (break the journey) | About 630 km · 10–11 h via Taroudant and Ouarzazate |
| Tangier | Via Fes · 11+ h (break the journey) | Not practical |

The lesson of the table: **plan an overnight stop on the way**. From Marrakech that means the Dades Valley or Ait Ben Haddou; from Fes the drive is long but doable in one (early) day.

## Option 1: Fly to Errachidia, then drive two hours

Errachidia's Moulay Ali Cherif airport (ERH) is about **130 km from Merzouga, roughly 2 hours** through the Ziz Valley and Erfoud. It is the fastest way to reach Erg Chebbi, and it makes a desert trip of two or three days realistic.

**Which flights exist?** Schedules at small Moroccan airports change by season, so treat this as a starting point and confirm with the airline:

- **Royal Air Maroc** flies between Casablanca and Errachidia, connecting with its international network at Casablanca.
- In recent seasons **Ryanair** has operated domestic routes to Errachidia, including from Marrakech, and **Transavia** has flown from Paris-Orly. Frequencies are low, often a few flights a week.

> [!WARNING]
> Never book a desert camp around a flight you have not confirmed with the airline itself. Small airports in southern Morocco may have only a few flights a week, and seasonal routes are suspended without much notice. Booking a refundable camp night helps.

From the airport, arrange a transfer with your camp or hotel, or take a grand taxi from Errachidia to Rissani and on to Merzouga.

## Option 2: By road from Marrakech

This is how most travellers arrive, either with a private driver, on a small-group tour or in a rental car. The route crosses the High Atlas by the **Tizi n'Tichka pass**, passes **Ait Ben Haddou** and **Ouarzazate**, follows the **Dades Valley** and the **Todra Gorge**, and reaches Merzouga via Tinejdad and Erfoud.

**Recommended pacing:**

| Trip length | Day 1 | Day 2 | Day 3 | Day 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 days (minimum) | Marrakech → Dades Valley (6.5–7 h) | Dades → Merzouga (4.5 h), camp | Merzouga → Marrakech (9.5–10 h) | |
| 4 days (better) | Marrakech → Ait Ben Haddou (3.5–4 h) | → Dades Valley (3 h) | → Merzouga (4.5 h), camp | → Fes (7.5–8 h) or back towards Marrakech |

The three-day version works, but the final day is around ten hours in a vehicle. If you can end your trip in **Fes**, or fly out of Errachidia, you avoid repeating the road.

## Option 3: By road from Fes

Fes to Merzouga is about **470 km and 7.5–8 hours**, through the cedar forests of the **Middle Atlas** (Ifrane, Azrou), the town of Midelt, and the palm-filled **Ziz gorges** south of Errachidia. With an early start you arrive mid-afternoon, in time for the camel ride to camp. Two-day trips from Fes (one night in camp) are common; three days with a night in a Merzouga hotel is more relaxed.

## Option 4: From Casablanca, Tangier and Agadir

None of these is a sensible single-day drive to the dunes.

- **Casablanca:** take the train to Marrakech (about 2 hours 40 minutes) or Fes (about 3.5–4 hours) and start your desert trip there, or fly Casablanca–Errachidia.
- **Tangier:** combine the desert with the north. Drive or take the train to Fes, ideally with a night in Chefchaouen on the way, then continue south.
- **Agadir:** travel via Taroudant and Ouarzazate, breaking the journey for at least one night. Our [itinerary planner](/itinerary-planner) includes a seven-day Agadir route.

## Option 5: By bus

Long-distance buses are the cheapest way to reach the desert:

- **CTM** and **Supratours** (the bus company linked to the ONCF railway) both run services towards Merzouga, including from Marrakech and Fes. Overnight departures are common.
- Buses are comfortable but slow, and arrive with no onward transport. Arrange for your camp or hotel to meet you.
- Book a day or two ahead in busy periods, on the operators' websites or at the bus station.

## Option 6: Organised tours

- **Shared small-group tours** from Marrakech (usually three days) or Fes (usually two or three days) are the budget option. They follow a fixed schedule and the camp is usually a standard one. Check how many hours you will spend in the vehicle and whether the camel ride is included.
- **Private tours** with a driver cost more but let you choose your pace, stops, camp and route, and are usually better value for families and groups of three or more.

When comparing tours, ask: how many people per vehicle; which camp, by name; whether dinner, breakfast and the camel ride are included; and whether the return goes to Marrakech or Fes.

## Reaching Erg Chigaga

Every route to Erg Chigaga passes through **M'Hamid El Ghizlane**, where the road ends. From there, it is about **60 km of desert track, 1.5–2.5 hours by 4x4**, to the camps among the dunes. A popular alternative is to leave the desert westward via the dry lake of Iriqui to **Foum Zguid** (about 3 hours off-road), then drive back to Ouarzazate, which makes a loop instead of an out-and-back trip.

- From **Marrakech**: about 7.5–8 hours to M'Hamid plus the off-road section. Most trips take three days with one or two nights at camp.
- From **Ouarzazate**: about 4.5 hours to M'Hamid. Ouarzazate airport (OZZ) has Royal Air Maroc flights from Casablanca and, in recent seasons, Ryanair domestic flights including from Tangier.

The off-road section must be driven by an experienced desert driver in a 4x4. It is not a self-drive route.

## What happens when you arrive at Merzouga

1. **Meet at a hotel or auberge** on the edge of the dunes in the afternoon. You can usually shower, store your main luggage and pack a small overnight bag.
2. **Ride out on a camel** (typically 1–1.5 hours to most camps) as the light softens, or go by 4x4 if you prefer or have mobility issues.
3. **Sunset from a dune**, then mint tea, dinner (often tagine and salads) and music around the fire.
4. **Sunrise.** Wake before dawn to climb a dune, then ride back to Merzouga for breakfast and a shower.

Camps range from simple tents with shared bathrooms to upmarket camps with private en-suite tents, electricity and heating. Ask exactly what is included: private bathroom, heating or blankets in winter, and charging points.

## What to pack for one night in the desert

- Warm layers for the evening, even in spring and autumn; a down jacket from December to February.
- A scarf or *cheche* to protect your face from sun and blowing sand.
- Sunglasses, high-factor sunscreen and lip balm.
- A head torch and a power bank.
- Closed shoes or sandals you do not mind filling with sand.
- Medication, and any snacks or drinks you want beyond what the camp provides.

## Common mistakes

- **Booking the Agafay as "the Sahara".** It is a stony plateau near Marrakech, not a dune desert.
- **Underestimating the drive.** Every desert trip involves long days on the road. Build in a night on the way if you can.
- **Arriving after dark.** You miss the sunset ride, which is half the experience. Aim to reach Merzouga by mid-afternoon.
- **Scheduling an international flight the morning after camp.** Leave at least one night between the desert and a long-haul flight.
- **Driving a rental car onto sand.** Getting stuck is common and recovery is expensive. Leave the car at your hotel.

## The bottom line

The fastest way to Erg Chebbi is a flight to Errachidia; the most rewarding is the road trip from Marrakech over the Atlas, with time to stop on the way. Whichever you choose, give the desert at least one unhurried night, and plan the journey out as carefully as the journey in.
