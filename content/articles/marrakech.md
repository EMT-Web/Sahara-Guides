Marrakech was founded in the 11th century by the Almoravids, a Berber dynasty from the Sahara, and became the capital of an empire that stretched into Spain. The city's red earthen walls, about 19 km of them, still enclose the medina, which is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Today Marrakech is a city of around a million people, a major tourist destination and the gateway to the High Atlas and the desert.

## Where Marrakech is and why to visit

Marrakech sits on the Haouz plain in west-central Morocco, at around 450 metres above sea level, with the High Atlas rising some 40 km to the south. It is about 240 km south of Casablanca and 190 km inland from the Atlantic at Essaouira.

Visit for:

- **The medina**: souks, palaces, madrasas and riads in a car-free maze of lanes.
- **Jemaa el-Fna**, the great square that transforms at dusk into an open-air theatre of musicians, storytellers and food stalls. UNESCO recognises its cultural space as intangible heritage.
- **Gardens**: Jardin Majorelle, Le Jardin Secret and the olive groves of the Menara and Agdal.
- **The base for everything else**: the Atlas Mountains, Essaouira and the road to the Sahara.

## The main sights

### Jemaa el-Fna

The heart of the medina. By day, orange-juice sellers, henna artists and snake charmers; from late afternoon, dozens of food stalls set up in rows and the square fills with musicians and crowds. Watch the transformation from a rooftop café first, then walk through. Performers expect payment for photos: agree an amount first or keep the camera down.

> [!NOTE]
> Monkeys and snakes on the square are part of an animal-handling trade that raises welfare concerns, and Barbary macaques are an endangered species. Many visitors choose not to photograph or pay for them.

### The Koutoubia Mosque

The 12th-century Almohad minaret, around 77 metres tall, is Marrakech's landmark and was a model for the Giralda in Seville and the Hassan Tower in Rabat. Non-Muslims cannot enter the mosque, but the gardens beside it are pleasant at sunset.

### Ben Youssef Madrasa

A Quranic school founded in the 14th century and rebuilt in the 16th, with a courtyard of carved cedar, stucco and zellige tilework around a central pool. It reopened after a major restoration and is one of the most beautiful buildings in Morocco that non-Muslims can enter.

### Bahia Palace

A late 19th-century palace built for a grand vizier, with painted cedar ceilings, courtyards and gardens. Go early: it is on every tour itinerary and gets crowded by late morning.

### Saadian Tombs and El Badi Palace

The **Saadian Tombs**, built in the late 16th century for the Saadian dynasty, were sealed off for centuries and only rediscovered in 1917. The Chamber of the Twelve Columns is exquisite but small, so expect a queue. Nearby, the ruined **El Badi Palace**, built by Sultan Ahmad al-Mansur in the same period, is a vast courtyard of sunken gardens and walls, with storks nesting on top.

### The souks

The covered markets north of Jemaa el-Fna are organised loosely by trade: leather, metalwork, carpets, spices, slippers, lanterns. Getting lost is part of the experience. The souks are calmer in the morning; bargaining is expected.

### Jardin Majorelle and the Musée Yves Saint Laurent

The garden created by the painter Jacques Majorelle from the 1920s, later owned and restored by Yves Saint Laurent and Pierre Bergé, is famous for its cobalt-blue villa, cacti and bamboo. The neighbouring **Musée YSL Marrakech** opened in 2017. Tickets are sold online for timed entry and popular slots sell out, so book ahead.

### Also worth your time

- **Le Jardin Secret**: restored riad gardens in the medina, with a tower view over the rooftops.
- **Dar Si Said**: a 19th-century residence now housing a museum of weaving and carpets.
- **Maison de la Photographie**: historic photographs of Morocco, with a rooftop café.
- **The Mellah**: the historic Jewish quarter, with the Lazama Synagogue and the Miaara cemetery.
- **A hammam**: from a simple neighbourhood bathhouse to a spa. See below.

## How many days to spend in Marrakech

| Time | What fits |
|---|---|
| 1 day | Medina highlights, Jemaa el-Fna at dusk. Rushed. |
| 2 days | Medina, palaces, Majorelle, souks, a hammam. Good minimum. |
| 3 days | All of the above plus a day trip to the Atlas, Ourika or Essaouira. Ideal. |
| 4+ days | Add a night in the mountains, a cooking class or a slower pace. |

## Where to stay

| Area | Best for | Consider |
|---|---|---|
| Medina (Mouassine, Bab Doukkala, Riad Zitoun) | Atmosphere, riads, walking to sights | Cars cannot reach many riads; lanes are confusing at first |
| Kasbah (south medina) | Quieter, close to the Saadian Tombs and El Badi | Further from the northern souks |
| Gueliz | Modern hotels, cafés and restaurants, near the train station | Less atmospheric; taxi to the medina |
| Hivernage | Upmarket hotels with pools, nightlife | Resort feel, taxi to the medina |
| Palmeraie / Agafay | Resorts and villas with space and pools | You will need transport for everything |

A **riad** is a traditional house built around an interior courtyard or garden, now often a small guesthouse. The best ones offer quiet, beautiful rooms, rooftop terraces and excellent breakfasts. In winter, ask about heating: open courtyards make some riads cold.

> [!TIP]
> Ask your riad to meet you at the nearest car drop-off point on arrival. Navigation apps struggle in the medina, and a staff member will walk you to the door.

## Getting there and getting around

- **By air:** Marrakech Menara (RAK) is about 6 km from the medina, around 20 minutes by car. Pre-book a transfer through your riad, or take an airport taxi and agree the fare before you leave.
- **By train:** ONCF trains link Marrakech with Casablanca (about 2 hours 40 minutes), Rabat, Tangier (with a change to Al Boraq at Casablanca or Kenitra on some services) and Fes (about 7 hours). The station is in Gueliz.
- **By bus:** CTM and Supratours connect Marrakech with most of the country, including Essaouira and the south.
- **In the city:** walk in the medina. Beige **petit taxis** carry up to three passengers; ask for the meter or agree a fare first.

## Food in Marrakech

Marrakech's signature dish is **tanjia**, beef or lamb slow-cooked for hours in a clay urn, traditionally in the embers of a hammam furnace. Try it in the small restaurants near the souks. Other things to eat: **mechoui** (slow-roasted lamb) in the lane known for it near Jemaa el-Fna; harira and grilled skewers at the evening stalls; and fresh orange juice from the square's stands. Our [Moroccan food guide](/culture-food/moroccan-food-guide) explains the dishes and how to eat well.

## Hammams

A hammam is a steam bath, a central part of Moroccan life. A **public hammam** is inexpensive and local: bring your own black soap, glove (*kessa*), towel and flip-flops, and follow what others do. A **spa hammam** in a riad or hotel offers a private scrub and massage at a higher price, and is a gentler introduction.

## Common mistakes and scams

- **"The square is closed" or "the souk is that way"**: unsolicited directions that end in a shop or a demand for a tip. Use an offline map and keep walking.
- **Henna that starts before a price is agreed.** Say no clearly, or agree the price first.
- **Taxis without meters.** Ask for the meter, or agree the fare before you get in.
- **Trying to see everything in the midday heat.** In summer, go out early and late and rest in the afternoon.
- **Expecting a "desert day trip".** The Sahara is at least 7.5 hours away. See [how to get to the Sahara](/sahara-desert/how-to-get-to-the-sahara-desert).

## Best time to visit Marrakech

**March to May and October to November** are ideal: warm days, cool evenings. **December to February** is sunny and mild by day (around 18–20°C) but cold at night. **June to September** is hot, with July and August averaging around 37°C. See [the best time to visit Morocco](/travel-tips/best-time-to-visit-morocco).

## Suggested 3-day plan

- **Day 1:** Koutoubia, Bahia Palace, Saadian Tombs and El Badi in the morning; lunch in the medina; Jemaa el-Fna from a rooftop at sunset.
- **Day 2:** Ben Youssef Madrasa and the northern souks; Le Jardin Secret; afternoon at Jardin Majorelle and the YSL museum; dinner in Gueliz.
- **Day 3:** Day trip to Imlil or the Ourika Valley, or a cooking class and hammam if you prefer to stay in town.
